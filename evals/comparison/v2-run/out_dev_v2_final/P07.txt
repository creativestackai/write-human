This study examines the effect of structured sleep hygiene education on the academic performance of undergraduate students. A total of 214 participants were randomly assigned to an intervention group or a control group for 12 weeks. The intervention was a weekly workshop covering sleep scheduling, caffeine management, and screen-time reduction. At the end of the period, the intervention group had significantly higher grade point averages than the control group (d = 0.31, p = 0.02), and their self-reported sleep quality improved as well. Sleep appears to matter for academic success, and the findings support adding sleep education to university curricula.
