This study examines whether structured sleep hygiene education affects academic performance among undergraduate students. Participants (n = 214) were randomly assigned to an intervention group or a control group for 12 weeks. The intervention was a weekly workshop on sleep scheduling, caffeine management, and screen-time reduction. The intervention group had significantly higher grade point averages than the control group (d = 0.31, p = 0.02), and their self-reported sleep quality improved markedly. Sleep appears to matter for academic success. The findings support adding sleep education to university curricula and call for further research.
