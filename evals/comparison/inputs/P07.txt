This study investigates the impact of structured sleep hygiene education on academic performance among undergraduate students. A total of 214 participants were randomly assigned to either an intervention group or a control group for a period of 12 weeks. The intervention comprised weekly workshops focused on sleep scheduling, caffeine management, and screen-time reduction. Results indicate that participants in the intervention group demonstrated significantly higher grade point averages than the control group (d = 0.31, p = 0.02). Furthermore, self-reported sleep quality improved markedly, underscoring the pivotal role of sleep in academic success. These findings highlight the importance of integrating sleep education into university curricula and pave the way for future research in this critical area.
