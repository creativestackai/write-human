At first glance, requiring two reviewers on every pull request appears to be an obvious way to catch more bugs. Upon closer examination, however, the picture is less clear. On the one hand, a second reviewer may notice what the first missed, particularly in unfamiliar parts of the codebase. On the other hand, when two people are responsible, each can assume the other has looked carefully, and reviews may become slower without becoming better. It follows that the rule is likely to help most where changes are risky and least where they are routine. Taken together, these considerations suggest that two reviewers should probably be required only for changes to payment and authentication code, with one reviewer remaining sufficient elsewhere.
