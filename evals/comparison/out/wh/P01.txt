Remote work is great at some things and bad at others, and most arguments about it go wrong by only talking about one half.

The good part is easy to list. You can work when you're sharpest, you get your commute back, and a company can hire the best person for the job even if that person lives eleven time zones away. The bad part is just as real. A message gets read in the wrong tone and sits there for a day. Someone goes a whole week without talking to anyone who isn't on a screen. And the thing that's hardest to hold onto is the thing nobody ever planned: the hallway chat where you found out how the team really felt about a project.

I don't think the answer is picking remote or office. That's the wrong fight. What matters more is whether people trust each other to get the work done, say plainly what's going on, and own it when something slips. Tools help, but only after that. A shared doc and a team that trusts each other will outwork a dozen apps and a team that doesn't.
