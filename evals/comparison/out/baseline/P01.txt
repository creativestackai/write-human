Remote work has real upsides. You get flexibility, people can actually fit their lives around their jobs, and you can hire the best person for a role instead of the best person within forty minutes of the office. The downsides are just as real. Messages get misread, people go days without talking to anyone, and the culture a company took years to build starts to thin out when nobody shares a room.

I don't think the remote versus in-person argument is the one that matters. Plenty of offices run on distrust, and plenty of remote teams run beautifully. What separates them is whether people trust each other, say what's going on, and do what they said they'd do. Tools help a little. Good habits help more, like writing things down, answering people promptly, and picking up the phone when a thread is getting tense. Without trust, no software will fix it.
