We wanted to know whether teaching undergraduates good sleep habits would show up in their grades. We randomly split 214 students into two groups for 12 weeks. One group got weekly workshops on keeping a regular sleep schedule, cutting back on caffeine, and putting screens away at night. The other group got nothing. By the end, the workshop group had higher grade point averages than the control group (d = 0.31, p = 0.02), a small-to-moderate effect. They also said they were sleeping better, though that is self-reported, so we hold it more loosely. The results suggest sleep education could be worth adding to university programs, but one study at one point in time can't settle that. It needs replication with longer follow-up.
