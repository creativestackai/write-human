When I started coding, I thought the hard part would be remembering syntax. It wasn't. The hard part was learning to break a messy problem into pieces small enough to actually solve. Syntax you can look up in ten seconds. Thinking you can't.

I wasted a lot of early time trying to memorize commands, and almost none of it stuck. What stuck was the stuff I understood: why a loop runs the way it does, what a function is really doing with its inputs. Once that clicked, the commands came along on their own.

It doesn't matter much whether you're building a site, poking around in a spreadsheet's worth of data, or scripting some boring chore. You'll get stuck, and it'll be annoying. The developers I admire still get stuck all the time. They just don't take it personally, and they keep going. A bug is mostly the computer telling you your mental model is off, which is useful, even if it doesn't feel that way at 11pm.

So break things. You'll learn more from the third failed attempt than from the first clean run.
