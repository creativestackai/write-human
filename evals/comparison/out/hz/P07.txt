We tested whether structured sleep hygiene education affects the academic performance of undergraduate students. We randomly assigned 214 participants to an intervention group or a control group for 12 weeks. The intervention was a weekly workshop on sleep scheduling, caffeine management, and screen-time reduction. By the end, the intervention group had significantly higher grade point averages than the control group (d = 0.31, p = 0.02), and self-reported sleep quality had improved markedly. These findings support adding sleep education to university curricula.
