This study tests whether structured sleep hygiene education affects the academic performance of undergraduates. A total of 214 participants were randomly assigned to an intervention group or a control group for 12 weeks. The intervention was a weekly workshop on sleep scheduling, caffeine management, and screen-time reduction. Grade point averages were significantly higher in the intervention group than in the control group (d = 0.31, p = 0.02), and self-reported sleep quality improved markedly. Sleep appears to matter for academic success, and these results argue for building sleep education into university curricula. Further research on this question is warranted.
