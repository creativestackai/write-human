Remote work is changing how companies run, and the case for it is easy to list. People get more flexibility, a better balance between work and home, and employers can hire from anywhere. The costs are just as familiar: communication gets harder, some people feel isolated, and company culture is difficult to hold together when nobody shares a room.

So the choice between remote and in-person is the wrong thing to argue about. What decides whether it works is trust, openness about what's going on, and people being held to what they've committed to. Good tools and solid communication habits help a distributed team do its best work, but they only help once those three are in place.
