At first glance, banning phones in classrooms appears to be a straightforward way to improve concentration. Upon closer examination, however, the question is more complicated than it seems. On the one hand, phones are a constant source of distraction, and removing them eliminates a temptation that few teenagers can resist on their own. On the other hand, phones are also a tool for looking things up, and a blanket ban treats every student as though they cannot be trusted with one. It follows that the strongest policy is neither a total ban nor total freedom. Taken together, these considerations suggest that phones should be put away during lessons by default, with teachers free to allow them for specific tasks.
