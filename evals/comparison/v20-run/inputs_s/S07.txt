This study delves into the multifaceted relationship between sleep duration and academic performance among undergraduate students. Leveraging survey data from 1,840 students across three universities, we found that students sleeping fewer than six hours per night had a mean GPA 0.31 points lower than those sleeping seven to eight hours. It is important to note that this association remained significant after controlling for part-time employment and course load. Furthermore, the effect was notably more pronounced among first-year students. These findings underscore the pivotal role of sleep in academic success and highlight the need for targeted institutional interventions.
