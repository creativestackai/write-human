Nashik should move to three 8-hour shifts, provided six additional pickers are hired before the December peak.

The obvious objection is that three shifts instead of two 10-hour ones means more handovers per day. The September trial at Nashik suggests that cost is outweighed: over four weeks, picking errors fell from 2.4% to 1.7%, which points to shorter shifts reducing fatigue-related mistakes. The trial also showed a problem. Overtime costs rose 11% over the same period, which suggests the third shift was not fully staffed. The six extra hires are meant to fix that.
