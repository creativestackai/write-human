This study examines how sleep duration relates to academic performance among undergraduate students. Using survey data from 1,840 students at three universities, we found that those sleeping fewer than six hours per night had a mean GPA 0.31 points lower than those sleeping seven to eight. The association remained significant after controlling for part-time employment and course load, and it was more pronounced among first-year students. Sleep appears to play a central role in academic success, and the results make a case for targeted institutional interventions.
