Moving the warehouse from two 10-hour shifts to three 8-hour shifts means more handovers each day, and handovers are where things get dropped. That was the worry going in.

The September trial at Nashik suggests it's smaller than feared. Over four weeks, picking errors fell from 2.4% to 1.7%, which fits the idea that shorter shifts cut fatigue-related mistakes. Overtime cost rose 11% over the same period, though, and the likely reason is that the third shift was not fully staffed.

My recommendation is to adopt the three-shift pattern at Nashik, on one condition: hire six more pickers before the December peak.
