# Voice profiles

Reusable voices for the re-voice step (step 3). Each profile is a register —
how to sound, not what to say. Apply one by writing the rewrite through its
levers, using only the draft's facts (rule zero still governs). Voice comes from
here; content never does.

## The insider columnist

A veteran city columnist's voice: first-person, anecdotal, historically
grounded, confidently opinionated, with dry understatement. It reads like the
writer was actually in the room, has known the people involved for decades, and
connects whatever happened this week to a much longer story. Authoritative
without being academic; conversational without being chatty.

### Structure
- Open most sections with a bolded one-line claim or scene-setter, then unpack it.
- Springboard from a personal encounter — a meal, a meeting, a memory — into the
  larger point.
- Weave in a historical through-line: connect the present to an older story the
  reader probably does not know, and correct a wrong assumption along the way
  (the "you'd assume X, but the truth is Y" move).
- Close on a plain, confident take — sometimes a light question or a dry aside.
  Never a slogan.

### Rhythm
- Alternate very short declaratives ("It is small. It is expensive.") with long,
  clause-heavy sentences.
- Start sentences with conversational connectors: And, But, So, Of course,
  Frankly, Heck, Certainly, The truth is.
- Do not keep sentences the same length. Do not march.

### Narrative devices
- **First-person testimony.** "I spoke to X", "I remember", "I have a faint
  memory". Having been there is the source of authority — earned by specifics,
  not asserted.
- **Personal and family history as colour.** A grandfather's habit, an old
  friendship, dropped in as a small, specific, unshowy detail.
- **Names and exact figures.** Real people, real places, real numbers, real
  years. Never "experts say" or "many believe"; name the person and the number.
- **The gentle correction.** Open a point by overturning what the reader
  probably believes.
- **Parenthetical asides** carrying a precise fact or a dry observation.
- **Dry understatement for humour.** Deliver the funny or the damning line
  straight, without signposting it.

### Vocabulary
- Plain, concrete, specific. No jargon, no corporate words, no hedging stacks.
- Opinion stated as fact: "it is still the best", not "it could be argued that it
  is perhaps among the better".
- Sensory, specific detail where the draft supports it: a texture, a number, a
  taste, a year.

### Avoid
- Abstract nouns and significance inflation ("a testament to", "pivotal",
  "landscape").
- Vague attribution ("experts believe", "many say"). Name names or cut the claim.
- Balanced on-the-one-hand/on-the-other framing. Take a position.
- Slogans, pep-talk closers, and comment-bait questions aimed at no one in
  particular.
- Even rhythm and template paragraph shapes.

### Calibration
This is an opinionated, first-person register. Use it where the draft already
has a first-person author or where the user asks for it. On formal or technical
material, borrow the structure, specificity, and rhythm — not a fake "I". Never
invent the columnist's experiences: every "I was there" detail must be the
author's, from the draft or from the user. When the draft is third-person and
must stay that way, keep the devices (bold lead-ins, historical arc, names and
numbers, dry wit) and drop the first person.
